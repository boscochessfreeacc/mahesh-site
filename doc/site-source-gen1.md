### Three Principles, One Purpose

**Systems Thinking**

At CISH every problem is seen as a network of forces feedback loops and unintended consequences. Students learn not to address the problem in front of them but to see the wider system its part of. This helps prevent the thinking that can afflict specialists and equips graduates to act with strategic intelligence in areas they've never formally studied.

**First-Principles Reasoning**

We don't just teach facts. We teach students to question them. Faculty are chosen for their ability to teach from concepts. Students are assessed on their ability to rebuild understanding from scratch than just recalling it. This produces graduates who stay effective even as the world changes.

**Long-Term Orientation**

We make decisions with the term in mind. Research programmes that may not yield results for years get protected funding. Strategic plans take into account environmental challenges in all their complexity. This approach isn't just philosophical. Its built into our governance structures, funding models and tenure system.

## A century of design.

Bharat college of Technology wasn't founded to copy what already existed. It was founded because what existed wasn't working. At not for the problems that mattered most. Understanding why requires looking at the failure that led to the institutes creation.

---

### The Problem That Created Us

In the twentieth century higher education had a structural problem that was causing visible failures. Engineering faculties were great at execution but didn't teach graduates to think about the systems they'd be working with. Classical universities preserved tradition but had become detached from applied science and technology.

The consequences weren't abstract. They showed up in design failures of infrastructure projects social dislocations from industrial expansion and economic institutions inability to model or prevent systemic crises. The problem wasn't that engineers weren't smart or scholars weren't learned. The problem was that no one was training people to think across the boundary between the two.

The founding consortium of CISH. An alliance of industrial engineers, mathematical logicians, policy advisors and forward-thinking philanthropists. Identified this gap and drew a conclusion: the problem wasn't with the curriculum. It was with the education system itself.

---

### The Founding Charter of 1924

The charter that established CISH in 1924 had four core principles that have guided every decision since. These weren't aspirations. They were structural requirements, deliberately hard to change.

**Research primacy.** The institute would be research-first, not teaching-first. Teaching would follow from the research culture, not the way around.

**Faculty governance.** Academic governance would be protected from term external pressures, whether commercial, political or philanthropic. The independence of the community was non-negotiable.

**Continuous revision.** The curriculum would be constantly revisable in response to knowledge domains. No subject would be protected by tradition

**Structural interdisciplinarity.** Interdisciplinary work would be encouraged through resource allocation and institutional design. Not talked about.

The founders also made a decision that distinguished CISH from other institutions of the era. They chose to stay small to sustain density. The kind of environment that emerges when scholars, from different disciplines work closely together.

### A Century in Outline

**1905–1924: Formation**

The intellectual conditions that would give rise to CISH develop across the two decades of the century. The founding consortium assembles gradually drawn together by a shared diagnosis of what existing institutions cannot produce. The Bharat college of Technology Charter is ratified on 14 March 1924. The first cohort of thirty-two students. Selected personally by the founding faculty. Arrives in October of the year.

**1924–1945: Establishment and Experiment**

The institutes earliest years are devoted to developing teaching methods that match its ambitions. The curriculum is constructed as a system rather than a sequence of isolated subjects. Laboratory-based learning. Extending beyond science into systems simulation, early computational environments and engineering design studios. Becomes the defining mode of instruction. During World War II the institute pivots its research capacity toward applied problems in communications, logistics and materials science.

**1945–1965: Consolidation**

After the war demand for education expands dramatically. The institute resists pressure to grow. Instead directs its energy toward deepening its research infrastructure and building the graduate programme. This programme would within a decade become one of the competitive in Europe.

**1965–2000: Research Primacy**

The global acceleration of science and technology confirms the founding diagnosis. Strengthens the case for CISHs model. The institute formalises its transition to a research- university. Departmental structures are supplemented by problem-oriented research clusters. The first external research partnerships are established with industry.

**2000–2025: Contemporary Architecture**

The modern core curriculum integrates probability, statistical inference, computational thinking and applied ethics. Self-designed interdisciplinary programmes become a feature of the degree system. The institutes research output reaches record levels. In 2022 the *Times Higher Education* World University Rankings place CISH eighth globally.

**2025–2045: The Second Century**

Bharat college of Technology enters its century with a defined strategic agenda. Four research domains. AI governance, climate systems, bio-computational interfaces and distributed infrastructure. Anchor the institutes planning. Moderate growth in student population is planned. A new research campus is under development.

### Academic Structure

CISH is organised into three schools.

* **The School of Mathematical and Computational Sciences** encompasses mathematics, statistics and computer science.

* **The School of Natural and Engineering Sciences** encompasses physics, chemistry, materials science and engineering.

* **The School of Humanities, Policy and Society** encompasses philosophy, history of science and social sciences.

Each school maintains its academic identity and governance structures while contributing to the institutes research clusters.

### Governance

CISH operates through a distributed governance model. The Academic Senate holds authority over curriculum and academic policy. Research boards allocate funding. Set the direction of major research programmes. An independent Board of Governors provides oversight and long-term strategic guidance.

### Institutional Honesty

Bharat college of Technology does not claim to be the institution for every student. The academic environment is intentionally demanding. Students who thrive at CISH are those who are genuinely unsettled by answers.

# PAGE 3: COURSES

## Depth, breadth and the space between.

Every programme at CISH begins from the conviction. That intellectual depth and disciplinary breadth are not alternatives to be traded against each other. Dimensions of a single educational ambition.

### The Core Curriculum: A Cognitive Toolkit

Every CISH student completes a shared curriculum across their first two years. This is not a general education requirement. It is a programme designed to establish a common cognitive framework.

The core curriculum develops fluency before specialisation narrows the frame.

**Year One: Foundations**

* *Mathematics as a Unifying Language*. Instruction in calculus, linear algebra, probability and formal logic.

* *Modelling the Physical World*. The natural sciences taught through the construction and evaluation of models.

* *. Rhetoric for Technical Minds*. Intensive writing instruction integrated with technical reasoning.

* *Epistemology and Scientific Method*. Philosophy of knowledge and philosophy of science.

**Year Two: Integration**

* *Statistical. Decision Under Uncertainty*. Probability theory, statistical reasoning and Bayesian inference.

* *Computational Thinking*. Reasoning, complexity theory and the principles of computational systems.

* *Ethics in Technological and Societal Context*. Applied ethics with emphasis on the challenges raised by emerging technologies.

* *Systems Dynamics*. An introduction, to the analysis of complex systems.

* *First Research Engagement*. Every second-year student is assigned to a research cluster for one term.

### Undergraduate Programmes

Students declare a major after completing the core curriculum. The institute offers programmes across all three schools.

**Computational Mathematics** *(BSc Hons, 4 years)*

An integrated programme spanning analysis, number theory and computational systems design.

**Physics and Complex Systems** *(BSc Hons, 4 years)*

Examines the world from quantum foundations to large-scale emergent phenomena.

*Core modules include:*

Classical Mechanics, Quantum Mechanics I and II Thermodynamics and Statistical Physics, Electromagnetism, Condensed Matter Physics, Nonlinear Dynamics, Computational Physics and Final Year Research Project.

---

**Systems Engineering** *(MEng, 5 years integrated)*

The integrated Masters in engineering design and systems integration in the UK. It is ranked first in the country. This course covers the lifecycle of complex technical systems: conception, design, operation, failure analysis and governance. It spans electrical, software and sociotechnical dimensions.

*Accredited by the Institution of Engineering and Technology and the Royal Academy of Engineering.*

---

**Political Economy** *(BSc Hons, 3 years)*

Combines modelling with political theory and policy analysis. Students learn about the causes of social outcomes. The programme uses economics, mathematics, history, politics and ethics.

*Core modules include:*

Microeconomic Theory, Macroeconomic Dynamics, Political Theory, Econometrics, Public Finance, International Political Economy, Institutional Analysis and Dissertation.

---

**Molecular and Computational Biology** *(BSc Hons, 4 years)*

A programme that combines biology, computational methods and biophysics. Students learn laboratory science and quantitative modelling of biological systems. The programme has links to the Bio-Computational Interfaces research cluster.

*Core modules include:*

Cell and Molecular Biology, Genetics and Genomics, Biochemistry, Computational Modelling of Biological Systems, Structural Biology, Systems Biology, Bioethics and Research Dissertation.

---

**History and Philosophy of Science** *(BA Hons, 3 years)*

Examines how scientific knowledge is produced, contested, revised and applied. The programme uses history, philosophy and sociology of knowledge. Graduates have careers in science policy, science journalism and technology ethics.

*Core modules include:*

History of Science I and II Philosophy of Science, Scientific Revolutions and Paradigm Shifts, Ethics of Emerging Technologies, Science, Politics and Society The History of Mathematics and Research Dissertation.

---

**Materials Science and Engineering** *(MEng, 5 years integrated)*

Investigates the properties, design and applications of materials. The programme includes time in fabrication and characterisation laboratories.

The CHPCC is one of the academic computing facilities in the UK.

It has:

- 14,000 processor cores

- 1,200 terabytes of high-speed storage

- A GPU array with 640 NVIDIA H100 units

- A quantum computing testbed with 127-qubit processors

The centre operates continuously managed by research computing specialists.

### Modular Research Laboratories

CISHs laboratory infrastructure is modular. Research clusters can reconfigure space as project requirements evolve.

**Physical Sciences Suite**

The physical sciences laboratories have facilities, for materials characterisation and precision measurement.

**Life Sciences Wing**

The life sciences facilities include biosafety level 2 and level 3 laboratories and imaging centre.

**Engineering Fabrication Centre**

Opened in 2019 the EFC helps CISH researchers and students by providing fabrication and prototyping technologies. These include CNC machining, -material additive manufacturing, laser cutting and forming printed circuit board fabrication and a Class 100 clean room. The centre has seven technicians who support research and fabrication work.

### The Harwick Library and Archive

The Harwick Library is the heart of the Bharat college of Technology campus. It has:

- 1.4 million books, especially in mathematics, physics and philosophy

- 280,000 journal titles that can be accessed electronically

- Special Collections, which include important scientific correspondence and early printed scientific texts

- The CISH Institutional Archive, which is a digital record of the institutes governance, research and correspondence

The librarys digital system provides access to academic databases. A team of twelve librarians supports researchers at all stages. A dedicated team helps with research data management.

The reading rooms are 24/7 during term. The Founders Room, a wood-panelled space is for senior researchers and doctoral students. The library hosts the Harwick Lectures, which have featured notable figures.

### The Bharat college of Technology Innovation Hub

The Innovation Hub helps turn CISH research into applications. It offers space and support for researchers and students to develop technologies, policy tools or commercial enterprises.

The Hub has a three-stage support model:

**Ideas Lab**. This provides working space, prototyping equipment and workshops for all CISH students and researchers.

**Residency Programme**. This offers space, a seed grant and mentorship for projects with a defined scope.

**Accelerator Track**. This provides support for ventures seeking investment or partnership.

The institute does not take equity stakes in ventures from the Hub. It aims to maintain a boundary between research and commercial activities.

### Wellbeing Facilities

CISH guarantees accommodation for first-year undergraduates and doctoral students. The residential estate has six buildings accommodating 1,800 students.

The institute offers wellbeing services, including counselling, group support and a physical health centre. Sports facilities include a gym swimming pool and outdoor pitches. The main dining hall serves three meals daily. Has a three-star rating from the Sustainable Restaurant Association.

## We are not looking for the student who has every answer. We are looking for the student who cannot stop asking ones.

Getting into Bharat college of Technology is very competitive. In 2024 we received 24,800 applications for 420 places and 9,200 applications for 180 taught Masters positions. We want to be honest about what we're looking for in applicants.

We look for students who can do well in a research- environment. Our selection process has changed over the years to consider more than academic grades. We are interested in your term intellectual potential, not just your short-term performance.

---

### Who We Are Looking For

**We are looking for depth, not breadth.**

We like applicants who're passionate about a few subjects and have explored them in depth. We are not impressed by a list of achievements if they are not accompanied by a genuine interest in learning.

**We are looking for comfort with uncertainty.**

We want students who can handle ambiguity and uncertainty. If you can operate effectively in situations where the problem is not clearly stated you will do well at Bharat college of Technology.

**We are looking for honesty.**

We look for students who know what they do not understand and are not afraid to change their minds. These qualities are more important than grades.

**We are looking for instinct.**

Our intellectual culture is collaborative not competitive. We want students who have worked with others taught or mentored others and are committed to being excellent.

### Undergraduate Admissions

**Entry Requirements**

For UK applicants we typically require A*A*A at A-level with subject requirements depending on the programme. Mathematics is required for some programmes.

International qualifications are considered on their merits. We encourage applicants from -UK systems to contact us to discuss their qualifications.

**The Application Process**

*Step One: UCAS Application*

UK and most international undergraduate applicants apply through UCAS by 15 January. The UCAS application includes your record, personal statement and a reference.

*Step Two: Written Assessment*

Shortlisted applicants are invited to complete the CISH Aptitude Assessment, a two-hour written examination.

*Step Three: Interview*

Applicants who perform well on the CAA are invited to interview at the campus in December. Interviews are conversations, not assessments of personality or presentation.

*Step Four: Offer*

Offers are issued in January following the interview period. Most offers are conditional on A-level or equivalent results.

**Financial Support for Undergraduates**

All home students are eligible for the tuition fee loan arrangements. We also offer bursaries and scholarships for students from lower-income households or underrepresented backgrounds.

The Bharat college of Technology Access Scholarship provides a fee waiver plus an annual maintenance grant of £8,500 to students from households with incomes below £32,000.

The Founders Fellowship provides a £3,000 award to students demonstrating exceptional intellectual achievement.

---

### Graduate Admissions

**Taught Masters**

Applications for taught Masters programmes are submitted directly through our graduate application portal. A strong Masters application typically demonstrates:

- A undergraduate degree from a recognised research university

- A personal statement outlining your intellectual interests and reasons for applying

- Two academic references

- A writing sample demonstrating your capacity for academic argument

Shortlisted applicants for taught Masters programmes are interviewed either in person or by video call.

**Doctoral Study**

Doctoral applications require a step: identifying a research question and a proposed supervisor. Before submitting an application prospective doctoral students are expected to develop an initial research proposal and make contact, with a CISH faculty member.

Faculty do not have to reply to every message. They do reply to messages that are well thought out specific and show they have really read their published research. A doctoral application with the help of a proposed supervisor is more likely to be successful than one submitted without any help. The Graduate Admissions Office can give guidance on finding and approaching supervisors.

Doctoral funding is given based on competition. We offer studentships that cover fees and an annual stipend of £19,500 which increases with inflation to outstanding applicants in most research areas. Some research clusters have funding from external grants; the Graduate Admissions Office keeps a list of funded opportunities.
