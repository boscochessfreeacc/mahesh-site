Admissions

We are not looking for the student who has every answer. We are looking for the student who cannot stop asking ones.

Getting into Crestfield Institute is very competitive. In 2024 we received 24,800 applications for 420 places and 9,200 applications for 180 taught Masters positions. We want to be honest about what we're looking for in applicants.

We look for students who can do well in a research- environment. Our selection process has changed over the years to consider more than academic grades. We are interested in your term intellectual potential, not just your short-term performance.

Who We Are Looking For

We are looking for depth, not breadth.

We like applicants who're passionate about a few subjects and have explored them in depth. We are not impressed by a list of achievements if they are not accompanied by a genuine interest in learning.

We are looking for comfort with uncertainty.

We want students who can handle ambiguity and uncertainty. If you can operate effectively in situations where the problem is not clearly stated you will do well at Crestfield.

We are looking for honesty.

We look for students who know what they do not understand and are not afraid to change their minds. These qualities are more important than grades.

We are looking for instinct.

Our intellectual culture is collaborative not competitive. We want students who have worked with others taught or mentored others and are committed to being excellent.

Undergraduate Admissions

Entry Requirements

For UK applicants we typically require AAA at A-level with subject requirements depending on the programme. Mathematics is required for some programmes.

International qualifications are considered on their merits. We encourage applicants from -UK systems to contact us to discuss their qualifications.

The Application Process

Step One: UCAS Application

UK and most international undergraduate applicants apply through UCAS by 15 January. The UCAS application includes your record, personal statement and a reference.

Step Two: Written Assessment

Shortlisted applicants are invited to complete the CISH Aptitude Assessment, a two-hour written examination.

Step Three: Interview

Applicants who perform well on the CAA are invited to interview at the campus in December. Interviews are conversations, not assessments of personality or presentation.

Step Four: Offer

Offers are issued in January following the interview period. Most offers are conditional on A-level or equivalent results.

Financial Support for Undergraduates

All home students are eligible for the tuition fee loan arrangements. We also offer bursaries and scholarships for students from lower-income households or underrepresented backgrounds.

The Crestfield Access Scholarship provides a fee waiver plus an annual maintenance grant of £8,500 to students from households with incomes below £32,000.

The Founders Fellowship provides a £3,000 award to students demonstrating exceptional intellectual achievement.

Graduate Admissions

Taught Masters

Applications for taught Masters programmes are submitted directly through our graduate application portal. A strong Masters application typically demonstrates:

A undergraduate degree from a recognised research university

A personal statement outlining your intellectual interests and reasons for applying

Two academic references

A writing sample demonstrating your capacity for academic argument

Shortlisted applicants for taught Masters programmes are interviewed either in person or by video call.

Doctoral Study

Doctoral applications require a step: identifying a research question and a proposed supervisor. Before submitting an application prospective doctoral students are expected to develop an initial research proposal and make contact, with a CISH faculty member.

Faculty do not have to reply to every message. They do reply to messages that are well thought out specific and show they have really read their published research. A doctoral application with the help of a proposed supervisor is more likely to be successful than one submitted without any help. The Graduate Admissions Office can give guidance on finding and approaching supervisors.

Doctoral funding is given based on competition. We offer studentships that cover fees and an annual stipend of £19,500 which increases with inflation to outstanding applicants in most research areas. Some research clusters have funding from external grants; the Graduate Admissions Office keeps a list of funded opportunities.

A Final Word

We know that the Crestfield admissions process can be tough. It is tough because admission to CISH is an investment. By the institute in the student and by the student in themselves. That both parties must enter with eyes. The rigour of the process is not designed to intimidate. It is designed to ensure that the students who arrive at Harwick Gate in October are genuinely ready for what awaits them.

If you have read this far and found yourself more energised than daunted by what we have described that is probably a sign. We look forward to hearing from you.
